# Growth Scan: High-Dollar Low Voltage on the West Coast

_Prepared 2026-09-26. This is a starting point to work from, not final advice. Check licensing and legal points with CSLB, L&I, BCD, or your attorney before acting on them._

## Bottom line

1. **The C-7 comes first.** In California a B (general) license generally can't take a prime contract for a single specialty trade like low voltage unless the contractor also holds that specialty license. Once the C-7 is issued, you can sign low voltage contracts directly with GCs, owners, and public agencies.
2. **You already have what most small low voltage shops don't:** W-2 crews, insurance, a GC license, a hazmat credential, and experience running multi-site rollouts on a tight schedule. Sell that.
3. **Relying on one customer channel is your biggest risk.** Aim to get 30–40% of revenue from outside MAPS within 12–18 months.
4. **Go after the markets where big checks are being written right now,** ranked below by fit and dollar size.

## Where the money is (ranked for The Impact Company)

### 1. Public works: K-12 (E-Rate), community colleges, UC/CSU, cities and counties ⭐ best fit
- **Why:** Contracts are awarded on lowest responsive bid, not on relationships. A new firm can win on price and paperwork. Budgets are stable and funding is committed before the work goes out to bid.
- **E-Rate Category 2** pays for structured cabling (Cat6/6A, fiber) in schools and libraries, and the C2 five-year budgets (FY2026–2030) went up about 20.7%. Districts plan these projects years in advance.
- **UC/CSU Job Order Contracts (JOCs):** for example, UCLA's *ITS JOC 2026-02* is an IT and telecom structured-cabling JOC. A single JOC award can pay out work orders for years.
- **What you need:** DIR public works registration, certified payroll (prevailing wage, usually the *Inside Wireman / Sound & Communication Technician* classifications), bonding, and ideally **SB-PW certification** (up to $43M revenue and 200 employees). SB-PW gets a 5% bid preference and access to the "SB/DVBE option" for direct awards. A DVBE partner or certification opens up more of this work.
- **Typical size:** $50K–$2M per project, and multi-year JOCs are larger.

### 2. Tier-2 subcontractor to data center and large commercial GCs 💰 highest dollars
- The data center construction market is about **$35B in 2026** and could pass $50B by 2028. Structured cabling nationally is a $13–16B market, and data centers are driving most of the growth. On the West Coast the activity is in Hillsboro OR, Quincy WA, Santa Clara/Silicon Valley, Reno NV, and Phoenix AZ.
- The major GCs (Turner, DPR, Holder, Rosendin/Cupertino Electric as electrical primes, and others) keep **pre-qualified sub lists**. To get on one you need track record, safety stats (EMR), and **BICSI Installer/Technician plus at least one RCDD**.
- **Realistic entry point:** don't try to prime a hyperscale job. Sub to the electrical or low voltage prime on fiber pulling, cable tray, and rack and stack, or provide W-2 labor on their projects. Your W-2 workforce and insurance are exactly what those primes check for.
- **Typical size:** $250K–$5M+ in sub packages.

### 3. Healthcare (hospitals and medical office buildings)
- California's **SB 1953 seismic deadline (Jan 1, 2030)** is creating years of hospital renovation and replacement projects. Each one involves large low voltage scope: nurse call, RTLS, DAS, security, and structured cabling.
- Hospital work requires HCAI (formerly OSHPD) processes and ICRA/infection-control training. **Your hazmat credential is an advantage here** because renovations in older hospitals run into asbestos and lead.
- **Typical size:** $500K–$10M+ for low voltage packages on major projects.

### 4. Multi-site rollouts for other national brands (outside McDonald's)
- This is **the work you already do**, for different customers. National rollout integrators such as Axseter, TechLink, Zare, Everon, Prime Retail Services, and Concerto, plus the managed-service providers behind retail, hotel, bank, and pharmacy chains, need reliable West Coast W-2 crews.
- **⚠️ Check your MAPS agreement first.** If the non-compete covers other QSR brands, target retail, grocery, banking, fuel/c-store, hotels, and healthcare clinics instead.
- **Typical size:** smaller per site, but a single program can be worth $500K–$3M per year across the West Coast.

### 5. Adjacent systems to add over time (higher margin)
- **Security and access control integration** (Verkada, Genetec, Avigilon, Brivo). Much of this work is recurring service revenue.
- **DAS / in-building wireless and private LTE/5G** for large buildings, which new fire codes increasingly require for ERRCS.
- **EV charger data and networking** (OCPP 2.0.1 is required on 2026 NEVI sites; California released $79M in a third NEVI round). The power side needs a C-10, but the network and communications scope can go to a C-7.
- **Fire alarm is *excluded* from the C-7.** You'd need a C-10 for it. It's worth considering later because fire alarm, security, and cabling are often bid as one package.

## Licensing outside California (West Coast)
| State | License for LV/cabling | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CA | C-7 (in progress) | No fire alarm under the C-7. |
| WA | Specialty electrical contractor **06** (telecom) / **04** (limited energy); techs need 06/04 cards | Techs need 4,000 hours of supervised work plus classroom training. Plan to hire WA-carded techs or use a local partner. |
| OR | Limited Energy contractor; techs need **LEA** or LET | LEA requires 6,000 hours of on-the-job training plus 432 classroom hours. |
| NV / AZ | NV C-2d (low voltage) / AZ ROC CR-67 or L-67 (check current) | Both are major data center markets (Reno, Phoenix). |

## Get on the bid lists
- **BuildingConnected** (Autodesk): this is where most large GCs send invitations to bid. The free sub profile is enough. Fill in trade codes, service area, licenses, and bonding.
- **PlanHub**: open-market bids, with good California volume.
- **Cal eProcure** (state), **PlanetBids / BidNet / BidSync** (cities, counties, school districts), and each **UC/CSU capital programs** site.
- **USAC E-Rate**: register as a service provider (you'll need an SPIN and a 498 ID) so districts can award you C2 work.
- Contact the **low voltage/electrical subcontractor prequal teams** at Rosendin, Cupertino Electric, Bergelectric, Sprig, Redwood Electric, Morrow-Meadows, and Helix. They sub out LV scopes and staff through labor partners.

## 90-day plan
| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Finish the C-7. Register with DIR. Build a one-page **capability statement** and a project portfolio from the McDonald's work (site count, schedule, zero-callback stats). |
| 2–4 | Create BuildingConnected and PlanHub profiles. Register on Cal eProcure and the major city and district portals. Apply for **SB-PW certification**. |
| 3–6 | Get 2–3 techs into **BICSI Installer 1/2**, and pick one person to pursue **RCDD**. Get at least one **manufacturer certification** (Panduit, CommScope, or Corning) so you can offer 25-year warranties, which many specs require. |
| 4–8 | Bid 10+ public projects (<$500K) to build a public-works track record. Pitch 3–5 national rollout integrators for non-QSR West Coast work. |
| 6–12 | Prequalify with 2–3 electrical primes on data center and healthcare work. Decide whether to pursue a C-10 (fire alarm and EV) or a WA/OR expansion. |
