# Action Plan: Growing from $1M to $3–5M

_Started 2026-09-26. Check items off as we go._

## Where we stand
- About $1M a year gross with 10–15 W-2 techs, so roughly **$70–100K revenue per tech**. Healthy low voltage shops often run **$150–200K per field employee**. That suggests the crew has **spare capacity** (or pricing is low). Either way, we can take on more work without hiring first.
- Nearly 100% of revenue comes through MAPS. Growing means adding customers, not just adding jobs.

## Step 3: 3–12 months (move up to larger jobs: data centers, offices, casinos)

### The realistic path into data centers
A data center's full low voltage package is often **$5M–$50M+**, with $10M+ insurance, large bonds, and union labor on many CA sites. We won't win one of those as a prime yet. **But one subcontract or labor contract on a data center can equal a year of revenue for us**, and that's realistic within 6–12 months if we do this in order:

1. **Supply labor or crews to the low voltage/electrical contractors who hold the data center contracts:** Rosendin, Cupertino Electric, Sturgeon, Black Box, Data Cabling Service (NW), and others. They often need extra W-2 crews for cable pulling, tray, and rack & stack. We earn while building the track record.
2. **Get certifications and references** from Step 2 (BICSI, a manufacturer cert, $5M umbrella, bonding).
3. **Bid scope packages** on smaller or edge data centers, colocation fit-outs, and enterprise server rooms.
4. **Get on the approved subcontractor lists of the big GCs** (DPR, Turner, Holder, Mortenson) through BuildingConnected.

**Where they're being built (West Coast):** Hillsboro OR (7 sites currently permitted or under construction), Reno/Sparks NV (Switch Citadel, Vantage 224MW), Phoenix metro (Mesa, Chandler, Goodyear), Quincy WA, Santa Clara/Silicon Valley. **Watch out:** WA and OR require state-licensed limited energy techs. Our WA partner helps there, but NV and AZ need their own contractor licenses. Reno and Phoenix are the easiest to add.

### Casinos
California tribal casinos are expanding: North Fork Mono near Madera ($400M), Graton expansion ($1B), Sky River in Elk Grove, Hard Rock Sacramento at Fire Mountain, and Eagle Mountain on the Tule River. Casinos are **surveillance- and network-heavy**, which is a great fit. Tribes are sovereign and choose their own vendors and requirements, so the way in is usually through the **GC or the surveillance/security integrator** on the job, plus tribal gaming-commission vendor licensing.

### Office buildings
These are mainly tenant improvements. Get on the bid lists of mid-size commercial GCs through **BuildingConnected and PlanHub** and bid the low voltage packages. Volume is high, jobs run $50K–$500K, and it's a good way to build references for larger work.

## Explaining "union shop vs. open shop"
- **Union shop:** the company has signed an agreement with a union (for low voltage, that's usually **IBEW**) and hires its techs through the union hall at union wages and benefits.
- **Open shop:** non-union. That's almost certainly what we are.
- **Why it matters:** many big projects (a lot of California data centers, large public projects under a **Project Labor Agreement / PLA**, and some casinos) require union contractors or union hiring. Open-shop firms can still win plenty of retail, office, public (non-PLA), and some data center work, especially in NV and AZ. **We don't need to decide this now.** It's worth revisiting once we see which big jobs we keep losing because of it.

## Money warning ⚠️
Big jobs pay slowly. Expect **net 45–90 day payment plus 5–10% retainage** held until the project ends. A $1M subcontract can mean paying **$200–400K in payroll before the first check arrives.** **Before landing a large job, set up a business line of credit** (and talk to the bank about an SBA 7(a) or CAPLines working-capital loan). This is the #1 way growing contractors go under.
